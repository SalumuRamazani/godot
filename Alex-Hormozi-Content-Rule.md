# The "2 · 5 · 10-to-1" Content Rule

*A clean write-up of Alex Hormozi's content framework, distilled from rough notes.*

---

## TL;DR

> **Don't give people 1 reason to do 10 things. Give them 10 reasons to do 1 thing.**

Pick a **single action** you want your audience to take, then stack reasons,
proof, and clarity behind it. One ask, ten angles.

---

## How the numbers fit together

The name packs the whole framework into a few digits:

| Number | What it means |
|:------:|----------------|
| **10 → 1** | **10 reasons** pointing at **1 action**. The core rule. |
| **2** | **2 levers** you can pull to change behavior: **motivation** and **ability (ease)**. |
| **3** | **3 content types** that carry your reasons: **stories, stats, explanations**. |
| **4** | **4-box value wrapper** — make the outcome big, then **fast, easy, and risk-free**. |
| **5** | Communicate at a **5th-grade level** so everyone understands. |

---

## 1. The core rule: flip "1-to-10" into "10-to-1"

Most creators make content that hands the audience **one reason to do ten
different things**. The result is scattered and overwhelming — nobody acts.

Hormozi flips it:

- Choose **one** thing you want people to do or believe.
- Give them **ten reasons** to do it.

One clear action. Ten ways of convincing them. This is easier to make, easier
to follow, and far more persuasive.

---

## 2. The job of content (for a cold audience)

With people who don't know you yet, a piece of content is a **motivating
operation** — a short-term nudge that makes action more likely *right now*.

You move the needle in one of two directions:

- **Decrease the bad stuff** attached to taking the action (risk, effort, cost, fear).
- **Increase the good stuff** attached to it (the outcome, the upside, the reward).

People want things they don't have yet. And the longer someone takes action
*without* seeing results, the more skeptical they get — so your content has to
keep tilting the motivation/cost balance back in your favor.

---

## 3. The 2 levers: Motivation × Ability

Every piece of persuasive content pulls one of two levers:

1. **Motivation** — give them the *reasons* (the "why"). This is where your 10
   reasons live.
2. **Ability / Ease** — make the action feel *doable* (the "how"). Make it
   **fast, easy, and risk-free** (and cheap, for B2B).

If you only raise motivation but the action still feels hard, people stall.
Raise both.

---

## 4. The 3 content types that fill the buckets

Your 10 reasons get delivered through three vehicles. Mix them.

### Stories
> "If we see X as someone who models Y and accomplishes what people want, we
> believe we can too."

- Use **real testimonials** as proof — e.g. *Sharon* as a real-life proxy for
  the viewer.
- **Hypothetical examples carry the same persuasive power as real stories** —
  so you're never short on material.

### Stats
- Numbers and data that **back up your claim**.
- "Look at the stats behind what I'm telling you."

### Explanations
- **How it works.** This is the type that **breaks a limiting belief** and
  replaces it with a new one.
- Explanation is also what makes the action feel *easy* — once people
  understand the mechanism, they can act.

---

## 5. The 4-box value wrapper: add "flavor"

Once you have a reason, make the payoff irresistible by running it through the
**value equation**. For each reason, ask:

1. **Dream outcome** — what big result does this lead to?
2. **Fast** — how do we make it quick?
3. **Easy** — how do we make it low-effort?
4. **Risk-free** — how do we remove the downside? (and **cheap**, for B2B)

This is the "wrapper" you pour each explanation into. **Speed sells** —
*"if you do X, you'll get the outcome faster"* is one of the most persuasive
angles you have:

- speed = stories
- speed = stats
- speed = explanation

---

## 6. Speak to the lowest common denominator

> **Write and speak at a 5th-grade level to reach the highest-level people.**

- It **forces you to be clear.**
- It's **faster for you** to make and **faster for them** to consume.
- Talking up loses people; talking plainly loses no one.

---

## 7. Make it actionable

> **"Don't talk about *stuff* — talk about *how to do* stuff."**

- Teach the **how-to**, step by step (e.g. *"how to build an SEO-optimized site
  with Claude"*), not vague concepts.
- Explain **how things work** to break an old belief and install a new one.
- Back claims with **stats**.

**Education = influence + a positive outcome.** When you teach someone and they
get a real result, you earn influence. That's why:

> **Selling = Stories + Stats + Explanation + Value Equation.**

---

## 8. Volume decreases risk

Making content takes time, but any *single* post is a gamble. The fix is output:

- Ship **10 pieces** instead of 1.
- More shots → higher odds that one **overperforms**.
- You effectively **10x your output and your chances of a hit.**

Volume is how you de-risk the fact that you can't predict which piece lands.

---

## 9. Description vs. Explanation (say something real)

A common trap: giving a **description** while thinking you gave an
**explanation**. They are not the same.

| Statement | Type | Why |
|-----------|------|-----|
| "He stole because he's dishonest (he lies, steals, cheats)." | ❌ Description | Just restates the behavior with a label. |
| "He stole because he stole." | ❌ Nothing | Circular — says zero. |
| "He stole because he has a history of being **rewarded** for stealing." | ✅ Explanation | Names a real cause. |

**Most of the time, people give descriptions dressed up as explanations.**
Good content explains the *why and how* — it doesn't just re-label the *what*.

---

## One-page cheat sheet

1. **10 reasons, 1 action.** Never the reverse.
2. Content = a **motivating operation**: cut the bad, add the good.
3. Pull **2 levers**: motivation (why) + ability (make it easy).
4. Deliver reasons via **stories, stats, explanations**.
5. Wrap each in the **value equation**: big outcome, **fast, easy, risk-free**.
6. **Speed sells.**
7. Write at a **5th-grade level** — clarity beats cleverness.
8. **Show how to do stuff**, don't just talk about stuff.
9. **Education = influence + positive outcome.**
10. **Publish volume** to de-risk and find your winners.
11. **Explain, don't describe** — give a real cause, not a relabel.
