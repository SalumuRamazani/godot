# Calibration example — the grading bar & house voice

This is a condensed real evaluation. Use it to calibrate: this depth of
specificity, this voice (direct, opinionated, decisive), and this severity of
grading is the standard. Notice that the script being graded *followed its
framework perfectly* and still only earned a B+ — because grading is on
performance and integrity, not coverage.

---

## The script under review

A tutorial: *"I Built a Site That Ranks on Google With AI (No Code)"* —
Hook → 3Ps intro → 3 steps (each with a personal story) → CTA to a free Skool
template. Tools: Claude + Replit + Search Console.

## v1 verdict — B+ (87/100)

> Well-*built* and framework-disciplined — ahead of ~90% of uploads. But
> framework-compliant ≠ high-retention, and one structural crack keeps it out of
> A territory.

**Scorecard (excerpt):** Title↔content `C+` · Hook `B+` · Intro/3Ps `B` ·
Teaching `A−` · Stories `A` · Credibility `C+` · Retention `B−` · Visual `C` ·
CTA `A−`.

**The crack (made impossible to miss):** The title promises "ranks on Google,"
but the chain breaks — proof is a *dentist* (a different project), the build is a
*water-damage demo*, and the payoff is "connect Search Console and **wait**."
Indexed ≠ ranked. The video never shows the built thing ranking → satisfaction
dip + the top comment writes itself.

- **Fix offered as two paths:** (1) *honest reframe* — retitle to "...Designed to
  Rank..."; (2) *earn it* — open a loop ("I built this 3 weeks ago, stick around
  for where it sits now") and end on a real Search Console screenshot.

**Other priced-in issues:** intro ~10% too long + salesy 8-reason stack;
"converts at 15–30%" and "$X from Google alone" are credibility withdrawals; no
open loops between sections; only one on-screen cue in an entire build video;
"Astro backend" is technically loose (it's a static-site generator).

**Protected (don't touch):** the three personal stories (specific, vulnerable,
belief-breaking), the "reference + conversion doc" insight (the real gold), the
textbook value equation, the clean single CTA.

**Final word:** "A B+ that's two moves away from an A−. Ship it — after fix #1."

## v2 re-review — A− (92/100)

The author out-executed the notes. Verify fixes *actually landed* (present ≠
working) and **hold the bar** — don't inflate for compliance.

**Movement table (excerpt):** Title honesty `C+ → A−` · Intro `B → A−` ·
Credibility `C+ → B+` · Retention `B− → A−` · Visual `C → A−`.

**Fixes confirmed with evidence:**

- Integrity gap closed *cleverly* — proof reworded from "this actually works" →
  "I **actually do this**," changing the proof's job from "the method produced
  this" to "I'm a legit practitioner." That makes borrowed proof defensible.
- Three open loops added and each *pays off* (the intro's "one thing nobody tells
  you… in step one" is closed by the reference+doc reveal — no bait).
- Stats reframed to defensible "double digits when traffic's qualified"; Astro
  corrected to "static-site generator"; on-screen cues now on every beat.

**New catch (own the earlier miss):** the script sells "~$20/mo" but needs
*two* paid tools — Claude **and** Replit (~$40/mo). Catchable; fix raises
credibility: "about 40 bucks a month in two tools vs a 2–3 grand agency invoice."
*This existed in v1 and the first review missed it — flagged honestly now.*

**Remaining polish to reach a flat A:** the $40 cost line; "Claude design" is a
vague product name; the ending is honest but soft (peaks in the middle) — bank
the end-reveal upgrade for when real ranking data exists.

**Final word:** "This is what a good revision looks like — you didn't argue the
notes, you out-executed them. A− now; fix the cost line and clarify the product
name. Record it."

---

### What this calibrates

- A perfectly framework-compliant script can still be a B+ — grade on
  performance and integrity.
- Every fix ships with a concrete rewrite; the #1 problem gets before → after.
- Re-reviews show movement, verify with quoted evidence, and refuse to inflate.
- Intellectual honesty includes owning your own prior misses.
