# The Framework (read before drafting)

Two layers stack here: the **structure** of the video, and the **persuasion** running through it. A good script satisfies both at once.

---

## Layer 1 — Video Structure

### Hook (first 5–10 seconds)
- A strong hook that **reinforces the title** — same promise, same keywords.
- **Lead with the outcome or the speed**, not your backstory. State the result, then tease the how.
- Short. One or two sentences. Create a reason to keep watching.

### Intro — the 3Ps
Grab attention by earning the watch in three quick beats:
- **Proof** — why you're worth listening to on *this* topic. Lead with the most concrete/visual proof (a before/after, a number you can show on screen). Order for impact; don't dump your whole résumé.
- **Promise** — exactly what they'll be able to do by the end. Concrete and outcome-shaped ("build a live site, no code, in one sitting").
- **Plan** — the steps you'll walk through. **The plan's steps must equal the body's teaching points** (usually 3). No mismatch.
- Keep the intro tight (~45–60s). It's the most common bounce point — don't over-pitch before you've earned trust.

### Body — List, Steps & Stories
- Introduce **three key points** (the "list").
- Break **each point into steps** (the "how").
- Share a **personal story** inside each point — specific, a little vulnerable, and belief-breaking (not a humble-brag).
- End each section with an **open loop** into the next ("…but a site Google can't find is worthless — that's step two").

### CTA (close)
- **One** clear call to action, to a **single** destination (free community / lead magnet / subscribe).
- Complete the thought — tell them exactly what to do and what they get.
- A genuine engagement hook beats a generic ask ("post your result in the community and I'll personally take a look").

### Scripted vs. unscripted
Either fully script and read from a teleprompter, or script the intro and speak freely through the body. Both are valid — mix to taste. (Ask the user which "mode" they want.)

---

## Layer 2 — Persuasion: the 10-to-1 Content Rule

> **Give 10 reasons to do 1 thing — not 1 reason to do 10 things.**

1. **Pick the 1 thing.** One action or belief the viewer should leave with. A cold viewer could act on it now. If there are several asks, split into separate videos.
2. **Stack ~10 reasons.** Across the whole video, give many reasons to do that one thing. Each reason either **increases the good** (bigger/faster outcome) or **decreases the bad** (less risk, effort, cost, fear). Don't dump all 10 in the intro — seed them where they're earned.
3. **Use three vehicles** to carry the reasons — aim for at least one of each:
   - **Stories** — real or hypothetical; both persuade. (Your personal stories double as this.)
   - **Stats** — a number that backs the claim. *Defensible only* — show proof on screen where you can.
   - **Explanations** — how it works; the kind that **breaks an old belief** and installs a new one.
4. **Wrap the offer in the value equation.** Make the payoff: a big **dream outcome**, made **fast**, **easy**, and **risk-free** (and cheap, for B2B). **Lead with speed** somewhere — "do X, get the result faster" is the most persuasive single angle.
5. **Clarity pass.** ~5th-grade reading level. Short sentences. Show *how to do* stuff, not just talk *about* stuff. Every "because" is a real cause (explanation), not a relabel (description).

---

## Description vs. Explanation (a clarity trap)
- ❌ "It ranks because it's well optimized." (relabel — says nothing)
- ✅ "It ranks because the heading structure tells Google what each section is about." (real cause)

If a "why" just restates the "what," rewrite it.
