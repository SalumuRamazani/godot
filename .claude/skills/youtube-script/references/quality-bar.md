# The Quality Bar (read before the self-audit)

Framework-compliant ≠ high-retention. Grade the draft on whether it will **hold a stranger's attention** and **survive the comment section** — not just whether it ticks the structure boxes. Be honest; a generous grade helps no one.

---

## Scorecard — grade each dimension, then fix or flag

| Dimension | What you're checking |
|---|---|
| **Title ↔ content honesty** | Does the body deliver what the title/hook promises? Any gap = top priority. |
| **Hook (0–10s)** | Clear, fast, concrete? Or abstract/slow in the middle? |
| **Intro / 3Ps** | All three present, ordered for impact, and *tight*? Not over-pitched? |
| **Teaching / structure** | Plan = 3 points = 3 sections, 1:1. Easy to follow. |
| **Stories / vehicles** | A specific, belief-breaking story per point. Stats + explanations present. |
| **Credibility of claims** | Will any stat/number get challenged in comments? Can it be shown on screen? |
| **Retention engineering** | Open loops pulling viewers between sections? |
| **Visual direction** | `[ON SCREEN]` cues on every step (critical for tutorials/demos)? |
| **CTA** | Single, complete, one destination, with a real engagement hook? |

Output the audit as **one line per dimension** with a grade and a one-clause note.

---

## The failure modes to hunt for (these are what cost the points)

1. **Proof → payoff gap (the #1 killer).** The video promises a result, then hands off to "later" or shows *borrowed* proof from a different project. A skeptical viewer notices the promised payoff never lands → satisfaction dip + "so it doesn't actually do X?" comments + lower back-third retention.
   - **Fix A (honest reframe):** retitle/re-promise to the real deliverable. Small wording, big integrity gain.
   - **Fix B (earn it):** open a loop in the intro and **show the real result at the end** (a genuine screenshot/number). Only if the user has the data.

2. **Over-pitched intro.** A long stacked-reasons "sales sequence" landing *before* trust is earned is where cold viewers bounce. Trim to the 3 sharpest reasons; move the rest into the sections where they feel earned, not sold.

3. **Shaky stats.** Suspiciously high or unsourced numbers invite "prove it." Either soften to a defensible range, **specify the denominator** ("of qualified visitors who reach the contact section…"), or show proof on screen for ~1s. Credibility is a bank account — don't overdraw it.

4. **No open loops.** Sections that just *start* have no pull. Add a one-line bridge at the end of the intro and each section.

5. **Weak visual direction.** A "build/demo" video with almost no screen cues is all *tell*, no *show*. Annotate every step with what's on screen (cursor, the prompt being pasted, the render, the deploy going green).

6. **Accuracy nits.** Loose technical wording hands "well, actually" ammo to your most engaged viewers (e.g., calling a static-site generator a "backend"). Tighten claims to what's precisely true.

---

## Priority fix order (highest ROI first)
1. Close the **proof → payoff** gap (reframe the promise, or add the real reveal).
2. **Trim** the intro's reason-stack and **add open loops**.
3. **Defend or soften** any shaky stat; show one proof screen.
4. **Storyboard** the screen capture for each step.
5. Fix **accuracy** wording.

## Don't "fix" what's already working
Protect: specific, vulnerable, belief-breaking **stories**; the one **differentiated insight** that's the video's "wow" (let the edit linger there); a textbook **value equation**; a clean single **CTA**. If the draft has these, leave them alone.

---

## Grading guide
- **A** — framework-perfect *and* honest, tight, with real proof and strong retention engineering.
- **B** — solid framework, but one structural crack (usually proof→payoff or an over-pitched intro).
- **C** — follows the shape but tells more than shows, or makes claims it can't back.
Aim every delivered draft at **A−**: every dimension at least solid, the two killers fully closed.
