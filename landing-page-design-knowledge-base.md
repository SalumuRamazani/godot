# Landing Page Design — Conversion Knowledge Base

> **What this is:** a distilled playbook of high-converting landing-page patterns for local & lead-gen service businesses (pest control, dental, legal, home services, etc.), compiled from a Google Ads conversion expert. It is written to be used as **reference context for an AI that designs landing pages.**

---

## How to use this document (read first)

You are designing a **high-converting, mobile-first landing page** for a service business that receives **paid search (Google Ads) traffic**. Use this document as your source of truth. When you generate a page:

1. **Start from intent.** Identify the visitor's search intent (emergency / location-based / research / price-shopping) and pick the matching pattern from §6 *Blueprints*.
2. **Follow the page skeleton** in §3.1, adapting section order to the niche.
3. **Pass the 5-second clarity test** (§2.1) before anything else — clarity beats cleverness.
4. **Design mobile-first** (§2.3). Assume 70–85% of traffic is mobile.
5. **Apply the copy formulas** in §4 for the headline, CTA, and offer.
6. **Self-check against the audit checklist** in §8 before finalizing.

**Defaults to assume unless told otherwise:**
- Primary goal = **lead generation** (form submission or phone call), not e-commerce.
- Target conversion rate = **20%+** (emergency/high-intent) or **15%+** (considered purchases).
- One page = **one offer = one primary action.** Remove anything that competes with it.

# Part 1 — First Principles of Conversion

## 2.1 The 5-Second Clarity Test (the #1 rule)
Microsoft's study of 200,000+ pages found the highest probability of a visitor leaving is in the **first 5–10 seconds**. If you communicate value clearly in 5 seconds, time-on-site and conversion rate rise.

- **Test:** show the page for 5 seconds, then ask *"What does this company do?"* Compile answers for accuracy.
- **"Grandma test":** someone with minimal tech literacy should understand the offer.
- Tools: Lyssna (UsabilityHub), or just friends/family.
- **Confusion is the most common reason pages fail.** Fix clarity before trust, value, or anything else.

## 2.2 The 8 Reasons Visitors Don't Convert
Diagnose in roughly this priority order:
1. **Confusion / lack of clarity** — most common; if they don't get it in 5s, they leave.
2. **Lack of trust** — without trust elements, nothing else matters.
3. **Perceived lack of value** — they don't see the unique benefit.
4. **Too much effort / usability friction** — the action is too hard.
5. **No urgency / motivation** — nothing compels acting now.
6. **Not enough information** — missing details needed to decide.
7. **Wrong audience / traffic** — even a perfect page fails on mismatched visitors.
8. **Psychological barriers** — unspoken mental blocks.

## 2.3 Mobile-First by Default
Most traffic is mobile (typically 70–85%). Design the mobile experience first.
- Stack key elements at the top of the nav/hero without overcrowding.
- Sticky **click-to-call** button or sticky CTA that follows the scroll.
- Touch-friendly targets; no horizontal scroll; fast load (< 3s).
- Background images often fail on mobile (text becomes unreadable) — blur them or drop them.
- Multi-step forms work on mobile **only** with clear progress indicators.
- Trust signals may need different positioning than on desktop.

## 2.4 Match the Page to Search Intent
Different intent demands different structure:

| Intent | Visitor mindset | What the page should do |
|---|---|---|
| **Emergency** (e.g. "emergency dentist") | Pain now, wants immediate help | Lead with phone; sticky call button; speed/24-7 messaging; minimal friction |
| **Location-based** (e.g. "roofers London") | Ready to act, comparing locals | Short 2–3 question form; local proof; area code/location in headline |
| **Research / cost** (e.g. "kitchen remodel cost") | Investigating, high-ticket | Longer multi-step form (feels custom); heavy trust-building; process & portfolio |
| **High-value / considered** | Wants reassurance before contact | Social proof *before* the ask; education; soft secondary CTA |

## 2.5 Message Match
The page must mirror the ad: keywords from the ad appear in the page, the offer matches the ad copy, and Dynamic Text Replacement (DTR) can swap the location into the headline/eyebrow per ad group (e.g. "Emergency Dentist in **[City]**").

# Part 2 — Anatomy of a High-Converting Page

## 3.1 The Page Skeleton (default section order)
Adapt to intent, but this flow works as a baseline:

1. **Promo / trust bar** — offer or aggregated review rating.
2. **Navigation** — clean, distraction-free; review count + click-to-call.
3. **Hero** — headline + subhead + visual + primary CTA/form + social proof.
4. **Offer** — sell the *offer*, not the service (e.g. "Free Design Consultation").
5. **Problem setup** — agitate the pain / stakes (esp. emergency niches).
6. **CTA pattern interrupt** — a secondary CTA mid-scroll to re-engage.
7. **Services / features** — what's included.
8. **Why choose us / benefits** — differentiators.
9. **About / team** — credibility, real faces.
10. **Social proof / testimonials** — reviews, before/afters.
11. **FAQ** — handle objections.
12. **Final CTA** — restate the offer.
13. **Footer** — contact, coverage area, compliance.

**Principle:** *awareness (problem) → reassurance (proof) → action (CTA)*, with multiple CTA touchpoints so the next step is never out of sight.

## 3.2 Hero Section (optimize this first — biggest impact)
The single most important element. Components:
- **Headline:** clear, concise value proposition; include the location for local services.
- **Subheadline:** adds context / handles the top anxiety (capacity, timing, price).
- **Visual:** high-quality image or video that supports the message (before/after is powerful).
- **Primary CTA:** prominent, persuasive, action-specific.
- **Social proof:** review rating + source (Google), accreditations.
- (Optional) **Secondary CTA** for a different intent (e.g. "View Projects").

Best practices:
- Don't cram — overcrowding confuses and kills clarity.
- Ensure text contrast against the image (avoid white text on light; blur busy backgrounds on mobile).
- Static images usually beat sliders (especially for older demographics).
- For pro services with few visual assets, use team photos / process illustrations.
- A real example: switching a hero from a *branded van* to *workers actively on a roof* lifted conversions ~5%. Show the service in action.

## 3.3 The Offer & CTAs
- **Sell the offer, not the service.** Dedicate a section to it (e.g. "Free Termite Assessment," "Free Design Consultation") with checklist-style benefits.
- **Lower friction / raise value:** make the offer feel risk-free and reframe commodities — "Free Quote" → "Custom Solar Savings Report" (explain what's inside).
- **Add perceived value:** bonuses (e.g. free stain protector), clear time delay ("1-day completion"), dream-outcome before/after imagery.
- **CTA copy is specific & action-oriented:** never "Submit." Use "Get My Free Assessment," "Calculate My Quote," "Get My New Kitchen Design."
- **Multiple touchpoints:** hero CTA, mid-page pattern-interrupt CTA, final CTA — consistent across mobile/desktop.
- **Match CTA framing to service type:** price-focused for commodities, value-focused for premium.
- Offer a **soft secondary CTA** (brochure/guide) for visitors not ready for the main action.

## 3.4 Trust & Social Proof
- **Place it as high as possible** — many visitors won't scroll far. Put reviews in the nav/header.
- **Stack it in ~3 places** throughout the page, especially near CTAs/forms.
- **Combine reviews with project photos** for visual services; pair testimonials with relevant sections.
- **Specific beats round:** "516 reviews," "30,000 successful jobs" are more credible than "500+."
- **Only show counts when substantial** (8–10+). Otherwise say "Rated Excellent."
- **Aggregate platforms** (Google, Checkatrade, Facebook) and show the source logo.
- **Real staff/owner photos in branded uniforms** beat stock; company vehicles add legitimacy. Match faces to expectations (e.g. mature, experienced-looking staff convert better for technical trades).
- **Accreditations/badges** must be perfectly aligned and professional — misalignment *reduces* credibility. Don't over-stuff the nav.
- Add locations + dates to testimonials/project photos; use full names + photos where possible.

## 3.5 Forms
- **Match form to intent** (see §2.4): emergency → short / phone-first; research → multi-step that feels customized.
- **Multi-step / two-step forms often outperform single long forms** — progressive disclosure lowers perceived effort. (But test; for older demographics single-step can win.)
- **Easy questions first** (postcode, service type, timeline) to build momentum; personal/contact info later.
- **Use dropdowns/visual selectors** over open text fields; combine first+last into one "Full Name" field.
- **Explain why** you ask for contact info ("so we can send appointment times"); phrase the phone ask softly ("in case we can't reach you by email").
- **Avoid technical questions** users can't answer (e.g. pipe length, roof height they don't know).
- First step visible **on the page** (no extra click). Clear next-step expectations after submit.
- Keep pages lightweight — too many form widgets/plugins hurt load speed and conversion. Tools: HeyFlow, Jotform, Unbounce 2-step.

## 3.6 Body Sections
- **Problem setup:** agitate stakes/urgency where appropriate (termite damage, emergency pain).
- **Benefits > features:** translate features into outcomes the customer wants.
- **Process / steps:** a simple 3-step "how it works" reduces anxiety about what happens next.
- **About/team:** real faces and specific expertise build trust for personal/high-consideration services.
- **FAQ:** proactively dismantle the top objections.
- Don't get lazy down the page — visitors who scroll past the hero are your **hottest** prospects. Every section earns its place; cut dead zones.

## 3.7 Thank-You Page (the overlooked multiplier)
Most marketers waste it on "Thanks, we'll be in touch." Treat it as a full landing page.
- **Must-have:** clear confirmation, next-steps overview, contact info, preparation instructions.
- **Enhancers:** personalization (first name), trust signals, scheduling option, a short video from the owner/team explaining what happens next, extra testimonials.
- **Why:** better-prepared leads → higher contact & close rates and better lead quality.

## 3.8 Footer
Contact details, service coverage areas, regulatory/compliance info, current copyright year.

# Part 3 — Copywriting

## 4.1 Headlines
A strong service headline tends to combine:
- **Location** (immediate relevance for local search).
- **Speed / availability** for urgent needs ("Same-Day," "24/7," "1-Hour Response").
- **The core service** stated plainly.
- **An emotional or outcome angle** ("Roofers who care about your home as much as you do").

Headline craft principles (from a high-performing example, "No more stairs…"):
- **Parallelism & rhythm** — memorable cadence.
- **Concrete visualization** — let the reader picture the outcome.
- **Emotional appeal** — name the physical *and* emotional pain.
- **Zero-risk bias** — "no more" / absolute certainty resonates.
- **Brevity** — passes the 2-second test.
- Balance clarity with persuasion — a clear, plain headline often beats a clever one. For location services, a sub-headline can carry the location so the main headline can sell value.

## 4.2 CTA Copy
Action-specific and outcome-framed. "Get My New Kitchen Design" > "Book a Consultation." Never "Submit."

## 4.3 Value Proposition
- State **specific benefits**, address the main pain point, give **evidence** for claims, and name a **clear differentiator**.
- Customer-centric language focused on desires and pains.
- Use industry "fear factors" where relevant (roofing → avoiding scams/cowboys; pest → property damage).

# Part 4 — Conversion Blueprints by Niche

Each blueprint = proven structure + what makes it work. Use the one matching your niche/intent as a template.

## Pest Control
*Source pattern: get.atomicmarketing.com/alpha-lp-pest-control · CR 17–30% · 75% mobile · 52% form / 48% call*
- **Structure:** Promo bar → Nav → Hero (headline + CTA form) → Offer (Free Termite Assessment) → Problem setup (instill fear) → CTA pattern interrupt → Services + features → Why choose us → About → Testimonials → FAQ → Final CTA → Footer.
- **Why it works:** fear-driven urgency + a risk-free free assessment + multiple CTA touchpoints + layered trust. Flow = problem → reassurance → action.

## Emergency Dentist
*Source pattern: unbounce template-emergency-dentist · CR 18–27% · 72% mobile · 57% form / 43% call*
- **Structure:** Nav → Hero → Clinic benefits + social proof → Services → Core offer (initial consultation) → Gallery → Meet the team → Final CTA → Footer.
- **Key moves:** click-to-call prioritized (phone top-right, "Call Now"); headline answers the 3 patient questions — *When can I be seen? ("Same Day") · Can you help? ("Emergency Dental Care") · Are you near? ("[Location]")*; minimal 1-step form; price de-emphasized (pain relief is the motivator) but "affordable" + starting price after value is established; layered social proof (aggregate Google reviews → testimonials near CTAs → team credentials).

## Junk / Rubbish Removal
*Source pattern: unbounce alpha-template-junk-removal · CR ~20% · 77% mobile · 76% form / 24% call*
- **Structure:** Nav → Hero → Sizing guide → Multi-step form (CTA) → Testimonials → FAQ → Final CTA → Footer.
- **Key moves:** before/after hero + DTR location; 8-step **HeyFlow** form that opens with an *image-based* question (visual, low-effort) and optional photo upload (qualifies leads, feels personalized); **visual size guide** using familiar reference points (bin bags) to self-categorize and justify price; dual hero CTAs ("Calculate My Quote" + "View Size Guide"); Checkatrade reviews in header; clean-up evidence photos answer the "will they leave a mess?" objection.

## Roofing
*Form strategy adapts to intent:*
- **Location-based (high intent):** simple 2-step form, 3 qualifying questions (service / property type / timeline).
- **Cost/price (research):** longer multi-step form (height, size, material) — creates a "custom quote" perception.
- **Trust:** reviews as high as possible; layer Google reviews + certifications + trusted-trader badges; combine testimonials with project photos.
- **Mobile:** sticky phone CTA; condensed info; touch-friendly forms.
- **Value:** lifetime material + 5-yr labor warranty, family-owned messaging, before/after imagery. Exclude email when phone is the priority follow-up.

## Divorce / Family Law
*Source pattern: get.atomicmarketing.com/alpha-lp-blueprint-divorce · CR 20–30% · 84% mobile · 64% form / 36% call*
- **Structure:** Hero (headline + form) → Reasons to choose firm → Process overview → How we help (benefits) → Meet the team → Testimonials → Final CTA (free consultation) → Footer.
- **Key moves:** **consultation-focused**, framed around the answers clients want (do I qualify? alternatives? expected results? timeline & cost) — answers, not pressure; qualification form positioned as "personalizing your consultation" with simple yes/no/unsure questions; compassionate, confidential, non-threatening tone; first-names-only testimonials with bolded key phrases; choice of call vs email.

## Emergency Services (drainage, locksmith, etc.)
- **Phone-first:** multiple prominent numbers, sticky call banner, every CTA = a call.
- **Immediate credibility:** review count + stars in nav, multiple platform logos, per-service completion stats, workmanship guarantees (counter the "cowboys" fear).
- **Speed/urgency:** "1-hour response," "24/7," "We come to you," simple 3-step process.
- **Price anxiety:** "best price guarantee," "no hidden fees," build call-out charges into the final price. Target 20%+ CR.

## Kitchen Remodel (high-ticket, research-driven)
*Source pattern: unbounce alpha-template-kitchen-remodel · long-form*
- **Structure:** Nav → Hero → Offer (Free Design Consultation) → Team + awards → Project spotlight + testimonial → About → Why us → CTA pattern interrupt → Our process → Project spotlight → Gallery → Reviews + testimonials → Reinforce offer + CTA form → Footer.
- **Key moves:** **bottom-of-page CTA form** intentionally — let high-ticket buyers (~$40k+) research first to capture better-qualified leads; sell the **offer** ("Free Design Consultation" with checklist benefits), not the service; DTR location; dual hero CTAs ("Free Consultation" + "View Projects"); information-rich, trust-heavy layout.

## HVAC
*Source pattern: unbounce alpha-hvac-template.* Follows the standard service skeleton — hero + offer + trust + services + reviews + CTA, mobile-first, phone-prominent.

# Part 5 — Optimization & Testing

## 6.1 The 10-Step Scrollmap Analysis Framework
1. **Find the 50% & 75% drop-off points** → move critical content/CTAs above them.
2. **Ensure key CTAs/benefits sit above drop-off zones.**
3. **Review engagement peaks** (where users pause) → add CTAs near them.
4. **Evaluate length vs. density** → if early drop-off, test a shorter/condensed page; move FAQs or collapse with "Read More."
5. **Segment by device** (desktop vs mobile) → mobile drops earlier; surface CTAs sooner.
6. **Match content placement to intent** → reorder so flow matches expectations.
7. **Investigate engagement-to-conversion gaps** → add CTAs right after engaging content.
8. **A/B test from scroll data** → e.g. move trust badges higher if few pass the hero.
9. **Correlate drop-off with bounce rate** → high bounce + early drop = weak first screen.
10. **Remove "dead zones"** → cut or replace sections with no engagement.

*Simple flow: find 50%/75% drop-offs → reposition critical content → align CTAs to engagement peaks → test page length → analyze by device → keep content relevant to scroll position.*

## 6.2 A/B Testing Methodology
- **Test one major change at a time** (start with the hero) — multiple changes = unattributable results.
- Get **enough traffic for statistical significance**; run longer on low-traffic pages.
- Document control + hypothesis + success metric. Test on relevant traffic segments.
- Don't blindly apply "best practices" — validate for your case. Start with **clarity**, then trust, then value.
- A page already at 15–20% may only need incremental tweaks, not a redesign.

## 6.3 Pricing Presentation
- Showing exact prices invites price-shopping → consider "10% off" / "save up to X" instead, to drive contact and enable value-selling.
- **Exception:** if you're the lowest-cost provider, showing price can help.
- Premium services: establish value *before* price. Commodities: upfront price can work.
- Price anchoring (higher then lower) is effective; build call-out charges into the final price.

## 6.4 Quality Score vs. Conversion Rate
- Add keyword variations/synonyms (e.g. via MarketMuse) to lift Quality Score — but **keyword stuffing can drop it**.
- **Don't over-prioritize Quality Score:** a QS of 5 with a high conversion rate is fine. Conversions pay the bills.

## 6.5 Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Too many widgets/plugins slowing the page; changing multiple things at once; ignoring mobile; untested CTAs; missing next-step instructions.
- Low-quality or generic stock images (can hurt trust more than no image); text-heavy sections without visual breaks; overcrowded hero; center-aligned large text blocks; making everything clickable; mixed/competing CTAs; sending traffic to non-optimized pages via nav links.

# Part 7 — Pre-Launch Audit Checklist

**1. First impression (5-second test)**
- [ ] Value proposition immediately apparent
- [ ] Passes the "grandma test"
- [ ] Headline communicates the core offer within 5s
- [ ] Trust signals look authentic

**2. Hero**
- [ ] Headline clear & action-oriented; subhead supports it
- [ ] Trust signals above the fold
- [ ] Primary CTA prominent
- [ ] Image supports (doesn't distract from) the message

**3. Visual hierarchy**
- [ ] Proper contrast; adequate white space
- [ ] No competing elements of equal weight
- [ ] Clear path: mini-headline → headline → support → CTA
- [ ] Mobile layout preserves hierarchy

**4. Trust & credibility**
- [ ] Recognized review scores; client/award logos
- [ ] Accreditations, years in business, guarantee/refund stated
- [ ] Testimonials with names/photos; contact info accessible

**5. Forms**
- [ ] Only essential fields; first step visible (no extra click)
- [ ] Progress indicators on multi-step; mobile-friendly inputs
- [ ] Clear value at each step; clear post-submit next steps

**6. Content & messaging**
- [ ] Benefit-focused headlines; scannable bullets; no jargon
- [ ] Specific benefits + supporting evidence + clear differentiator

**7. Technical**
- [ ] Loads < 3s; images optimized; no broken links/forms
- [ ] Mobile: adequate touch targets, no horizontal scroll, readable

**8. Traffic alignment & analytics**
- [ ] Ad keywords appear on page; offer matches ad
- [ ] Heatmap + session recording + conversion tracking + A/B capability live

# Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet
1. **Clarity first** — pass the 5-second test or nothing else matters.
2. **One page, one offer, one primary action.**
3. **Mobile-first** — 70–85% of traffic; sticky CTA / click-to-call.
4. **Match structure to intent** — emergency = phone-first; research = multi-step + trust.
5. **Sell the offer, not the service** — reframe & de-risk it.
6. **Trust high & often** — reviews in the nav, near every CTA, ~3 places; specific numbers, real faces.
7. **Forms: easy questions first, explain the ask, dropdowns over text, multi-step for considered buys.**
8. **CTAs are specific** — "Get My Free Assessment," never "Submit."
9. **Optimize the hero first** — biggest lever.
10. **Don't neglect the thank-you page** — it's a lead-quality multiplier.
11. **Test one change at a time**, start with clarity, validate with data.
